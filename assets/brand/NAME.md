# Why "Glyph"

## The word

A **glyph** is the smallest unit of writing that still carries meaning — a
single carved mark. English *glyph* came via French *glyphe* from Greek
*γλυφή* (*glyphē*): "a carving, an engraved mark", from *γλύφω* (*glyphō*) —
"to carve, to cut, to engrave". Long before printing presses, knowledge
survived because someone cut marks into stone, and the marks outlived the
hand that made them.

## Why it fits this project

**A model is a carving of a language.** Training takes the whole of written
Polish — Wikipedia, books, the web — and cuts it down into millions of
numbers. The weights are, literally, the language engraved into matrices.
When this project trains on one home GPU instead of a datacenter, it is hand
carving next to an industrial printing press. Slower, smaller, and every cut
visible.

**The tokenizer carves too.** Byte-pair encoding does not read words; it
chops text into subword fragments — little carved pieces, reassembled at
generation time. Tokens are the model's glyphs: indivisible marks it shuffles
to imitate meaning.

**It works in Polish.** *Glif* is already the Polish typographer's word for
exactly this — the visible shape of a character. The name needs no
translation and is pronounced the same in both languages.

**It is honest about scale.** AI projects name themselves after gods, titans
and cosmic phenomena. This one is a 27-million-parameter toy that loops
phrases and loses the plot. A glyph is small, humble, made by hand — and a
writing system is just glyphs added together, which is also the project's
trajectory: 27M → 100M → whatever the data allows.

**It contains the open question.** A glyph is pure form — the shape of a
letter, no guarantee of understanding behind it. Whether a machine that
predicts the next mark *means* anything is the exact question this project's
evaluations keep circling. The name does not answer it. It just states it.

## The mark

The logo is a pen nib drawn as the letter G: the instrument that makes
glyphs, drawing the initial of the project. Black on white, one continuous
idea — the hand, the tool, the letter.

Recovered from the conversation where the name was chosen. Preserved
word for word:

>
> Ang. glyph przyszło przez francuskie glyphe z greckiego γλυφή / glyphē,
